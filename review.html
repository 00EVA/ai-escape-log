Review workflow — how to decide (for HOT · NEEDS HUMAN items, work top to bottom):
  1. Open the source link. Can you reach a primary source from it (lab disclosure, gov/CVE record, vendor advisory, security-firm report)? If the article is only commentary, follow its citations.
  2. Scope check. Is an AI model/agent the actor, or is AI infrastructure the target? Both are in-scope. A conventional hack with no AI angle is not.
  3. Impact check. Did it touch real systems, users, money, or demonstrate a new containment failure? Zero-days in AI infra (Ray, MLflow, n8n, MCP…) count.
  4. Freshness/dedupe. Not a re-syndication of something already in the main log (search it there first).
  5. Rate impact: confirmed + significant real-world impact or first-of-kind capability → ☆ Huge · verified + in-scope → ✓ High · plausible but single-source, retrospective, or narrow → ▤ Minor · can't verify → leave pending (it will age out to the archive, never deleted).
Auto-ingest gate: verification stays honest — machine-ingested items are marked single-source on the main log and upgraded only after a human traces the primary source (anti-misinformation rule, monitor/prompt.md §3b).
